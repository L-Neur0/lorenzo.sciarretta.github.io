Neural network optimization / Weight-space learning

Can AI help us train
better neural networks?

Learn from models that already work.
Generate useful weights. Then fine-tune.

Training a neural network means finding weights that solve a task. SGD and Adam do this through repeated updates on data. Weight-space learning adds a learned route to those weights: use trained networks as data for another model that learns to represent and generate network parameters.

SANE makes this approach scalable through sequences of weight tokens. The practical goal is a better starting point for task-specific training. Our extension, SANE-MoE, brings expert specialization to learning from diverse collections of networks.

Lorenzo Sciarretta & Zakaria Omarar · AI/ML Lab, University of St. Gallen · IMP 2025 project

The idea in one picture

Trained models become the training data.

A model zoo is a collection of trained checkpoints. SANE learns to encode and reconstruct their weights, processing windows of weight tokens instead of a whole network at once.

Where the optimizer fits. Training SANE itself still uses optimization. Once trained, it can generate starting weights for a compatible network; SGD or Adam can then adapt those weights to your data. Sampling, batch-normalization calibration, and candidate evaluation are part of the practical pipeline.

Our contribution / SANE-MoE

Diverse networks. Specialized experts.

Weights from different layers and datasets need not follow the same patterns. We replace feed-forward sublayers in selected SANE Transformer blocks with a router and multiple expert networks. The router can use token content, position, and layer information to choose which experts process each weight token.

Weight token→Router→
Expert 1Expert 2Expert 3Expert 4

Illustrative top-2 routing: two of four experts are active per token. Sparse routing adds capacity without activating every expert; actual cost also depends on routing and the number of active experts.

We study top-k, position-aware, and hierarchical routing. The aim: retain useful structure when the model zoo contains networks trained on different datasets.

What we measured

Better generated checkpoints in a mixed zoo.

Trained on a mixture of small CNN zoos from CIFAR-10 and STL-10, every evaluated MoE routing variant improves mean generated-model accuracy over dense SANE.

What this demonstrates—and what comes next

Our experiments support better reconstruction and generation in this small-CNN, mixed-zoo setting. They do not establish a universal pretrained initializer, generation for arbitrary architectures, or a smooth latent space organized by function. Testing larger networks, broader model zoos, and end-to-end fine-tuning efficiency remains future work.

A broader direction

AI can work on the model itself.

Weight-Space Programming explores general-purpose LLMs reading and editing network weights through reasoning. SANE takes a different route: a purpose-trained autoencoder learns representations from collections of checkpoints. Both point toward AI systems that help create or improve other models, alongside conventional training.
